TRIAGE FIRST

Check urgency before tracking.

Before tracking, check whether the change is sudden, severe or worsening. Inability to urinate, collapse, severe weakness, repeated vomiting, marked dehydration, or severe distress needs urgent veterinary care. Recording can support a veterinary conversation, but it must not delay care.

Measure one normal day

Measure how much water your dog drinks in 24 hours.

Measure what goes into the bowl and what remains after 24 hours, accounting for spills and other animals.

  1. Use the same bowl and measuring cup for one ordinary day.
  2. Record refill times, urine frequency, accidents, appetite, and energy.
  3. Never restrict water unless a veterinarian specifically directs it.

Pair water with the whole routine

You will know which related signs belong in the same record.

Thirst becomes more informative when paired with urine, appetite, weight, vomiting, medicines, and energy.

  1. Note larger urine clumps, longer urination, urgency, or accidents.
  2. Add recent medicines, food changes, heat, and activity.
  3. Use a recent weight when available; do not force extra weigh-ins.
Which paired detail belongs in the same note?

Make the call specific

You will have a measured timeline and know the urgent exceptions.

Report when it started, approximate intake, urine changes, appetite, energy, medicines, and any vomiting.

  1. Ask how soon the dog should be examined and what samples may be useful.
  2. Mention conditions such as diabetes, kidney disease, or hormone disorders.
  3. Inability to urinate, collapse, severe weakness, or distress needs urgent care.
What should lead the call?

Share a measured water and bathroom summary

Connect water intake with urination, medicines, appetite and energy.

A measured daily pattern is more useful than an impression—especially after a medicine or food change.

  1. Measure how much water is added and left over during the same 24-hour window.
  2. Record urination frequency, accidents, appetite, energy and any vomiting or diarrhea.
  3. Share the dog's weight, current medicines, start date and the measured pattern with the veterinary team.
