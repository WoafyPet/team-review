TRIAGE FIRST

Check urgency before tracking.

Before tracking, check whether the change is sudden, severe or worsening. Inability to urinate, repeated unproductive straining, blood with weakness, collapse, severe pain, or a swollen painful abdomen needs urgent veterinary care. Recording can support a veterinary conversation, but it must not delay care.

Record the accident without blame

You will know the time, sequence, and body clues that matter.

Note when it happened, the last bathroom trip, route, urgency, posture, amount, appearance, and whether the dog seemed aware or distressed.

  1. Separate urine, stool, leakage, urgency, and inability to reach the door.
  2. Add water, meals, medicines, sleep, mobility, and recent routine changes.
  3. Do not punish, restrict water, or delay an urgent call to finish a log.

Make the route easier

You will reduce distance and slipping while the cause is evaluated.

Offer more frequent access and make the familiar route safer without changing every cue.

  1. Add traction, lighting, and a shorter route when possible.
  2. Offer calm, more frequent opportunities at predictable times.
  3. Use washable protection while preserving dignity and normal access.
What is a safe immediate support?

Know what needs faster care

You will be ready to report urgency, output, pain, and paired changes.

Bathroom changes can involve mobility, infection, pain, medication, digestion, or other conditions. Let the veterinary team evaluate the cause.

  1. Report frequency, amount, color, straining, pain, thirst, vomiting, and energy.
  2. List medicines and conditions, plus any recent diet or schedule change.
  3. Inability to urinate, repeated straining, collapse, or severe pain is urgent.
Which sign should not wait for a tracker?

Prepare a bathroom pattern for the care team

Connect timing, urgency, posture, output and the route to the accident.

Accidents can reflect very different problems. The details before and after the event help narrow the next questions.

  1. Record time, last successful trip, urgency, posture, straining, amount and appearance.
  2. Note route length, slipping, stairs, sleep, water intake and medicine changes.
  3. Share photographs only when safe and useful, plus the full event sequence and frequency.
