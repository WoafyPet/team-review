Some changes cannot wait.

Breathing difficulty, collapse, severe distress or a sudden inability to stand need urgent veterinary help. Call your vet for new or worsening symptoms. Never delay care to collect more notes.

Stiff after a nap?

Film your senior dog getting up on their own. Capture the floor under their paws and their first few steps.

Notice
Watch the first five steps after rest, slipping, stairs and recovery.
Help today
Add traction, shorten difficult routes and record one natural rise.
Record & share
Save a 10–20 second natural-rise video plus the surface, pause, first five steps and recovery time.
Call your vet
Sudden inability to stand, dragging a limb or severe pain.
Stiffness after rest in older dogs

Waking or pacing at night?

Note what woke your dog, whether they needed to go outside, and how long it took them to settle.

Notice
Record wake time, pacing, breathing, toileting and what helps settling.
Help today
Keep water, a clear bathroom route and gentle night lighting available.
Record & share
If your veterinary team says home monitoring is safe, record wake time, first behavior, bathroom trip, breathing and what helped settling.
Call your vet
Breathing difficulty, collapse or severe disorientation.
Nighttime restlessness in older dogs

Leaving food in the bowl?

Measure what your dog ate and watch for dropped food or chewing on one side.

Notice
Measure what was offered and eaten; note chewing, nausea and weight.
Help today
Keep food familiar and bowls easy to reach while arranging care.
Record & share
Record food offered, amount eaten, chewing side, dropped food, nausea clues and a dated weight when available.
Call your vet
Repeated vomiting, a swollen abdomen or refusing all food with weakness.
Appetite changes in older dogs

Drinking more water?

Keep water available. Measure how much your dog drinks and note any extra bathroom trips.

Notice
Watch for drinking more or less, a bowl left untouched, or needing to urinate more often.
Help today
Keep fresh water available and add easier bathroom access.
Record & share
Measure one ordinary day's intake and pair it with urination, appetite, medicines and energy changes.
Call your vet
Inability to urinate, collapse or severe distress.
Increased thirst in older dogs

Having accidents indoors?

Note whether your dog leaked while resting, asked to go out, or struggled to reach the door.

Notice
Record time, urgency, posture, amount, route and signs of pain.
Help today
Offer more frequent access on a short, well-lit, non-slip route.
Record & share
Track time, urgency, posture, output, accidents and the exact route your dog must travel.
Call your vet
Repeated straining without output, blood with weakness or severe pain.
Bathroom accidents in older dogs

Skipping a favorite walk?

Record where your dog turns back and which shorter outings they still enjoy.

Notice
Name the exact routine your dog no longer starts, joins or finishes.
Help today
Offer a shorter, lower-effort version and let your dog choose.
Record & share
Name the routine that changed, how often it happens, what your dog still chooses and what lowers the effort.
Call your vet
Sudden confusion, seizure, collapse or rapid major behavior change.
Loss of interest in favorite activities

One week. Clearer notes.

Start only after urgent signs are ruled out, or your veterinary team recommends home monitoring.

  1. Day 1 · Record an ordinary moment.

    A natural rise, meal or night waking. Do not prompt the behavior.

  2. Days 2–3 · Compare the same routine.

    Note the time, effort, frequency and recovery.

  3. Days 4–5 · Make one safe change.

    Add traction or easier access. Record what helps.

  4. Day 6 · Bring the details together.

    Sleep, appetite, water, bathroom habits and medicines.

  5. Day 7 · Prepare for your vet.

    What changed? When did it start? How often does it happen? What is harder now?

If symptoms worsen, stop monitoring and call your veterinarian.
